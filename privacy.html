IT TASKFLOW · PRIVACY POLICY

Understanding how information is handled

IT TaskFlow is being developed as a unified workspace concept for organizations, managers, project teams, and employees. The intended platform may support organization registration, employee onboarding, project assignments, task tracking, team communication, notifications, reporting, and additional productivity features. These capabilities can involve different types of information, and the information needed for one feature may differ from that needed for another.

This document establishes a proposed privacy framework for that product concept. It distinguishes information that an organization may provide from information an individual may submit and from technical information a service may generate. It also explains that an actual deployment must identify the responsible legal entity, applicable laws, service providers, and the real technical practices in place before this policy can be treated as a complete statement of practice.

01

PURPOSE AND APPLICATION

Introduction and scope

1.1 Purpose of this Privacy Policy

This Privacy Policy describes the proposed principles governing the handling of information in connection with IT TaskFlow, including its website, account registration experience, organization workspaces, project management interfaces, task management functionality, support channels, and related services that may be introduced in the future. Its purpose is to explain what categories of information may be involved, why that information may be needed, who may be permitted to access it, how long it may be retained, and what choices or rights may be available to affected individuals.

Privacy is not limited to preventing unauthorized access to a database. It also concerns whether information is collected for a legitimate purpose, whether the amount collected is appropriate, whether access is restricted to people who need it, whether information remains accurate, and whether individuals receive understandable explanations about its use. IT TaskFlow is intended to follow these principles as the project develops, subject to the actual product design, technical implementation, applicable legal requirements, and agreements with customers.

1.2 Services covered by this document

The intended scope includes the public website, sign-up and sign-in pages, organization and employee account experiences, project workspaces, task assignments, comments, notifications, reports, help documentation, and support requests. If additional functionality is introduced, such as interview management, recruitment workflows, automated testing, AI-assisted analysis, or operational insights, the information processed by those features must be assessed before they are released to real users.

A feature appearing in a demonstration, design, roadmap, or portfolio screenshot does not mean that it is already available as a production service. This policy must be updated where necessary to reflect actual functionality. Where a separate product, service, customer contract, or integration has its own applicable privacy terms, those terms should be reviewed together with this document to determine which provisions apply.

1.3 Who should read this policy

This document is intended for organizations considering IT TaskFlow, authorized organization administrators, managers, project managers, employees, invited collaborators, website visitors, and people who contact the service. Different sections may apply differently depending on whether a person merely visits the public website, creates an account independently, receives an account from an employer, submits a support request, or participates in a project managed by an organization.

Organizations should communicate relevant privacy information to their employees and other people whose information they submit to the platform. Individuals should read the sections that apply to their activities and contact their organization or the designated service privacy contact if they need clarification about who controls a particular record or how a specific feature uses information.

02

ACCOUNTABILITY

Who is responsible for information

2.1 The role of the IT TaskFlow service operator

Before IT TaskFlow is offered commercially, the person or legal entity operating the service must be clearly identified. That operator may determine the purposes and methods for processing certain information, such as website security logs, account administration, platform diagnostics, service communications, and support records. The operator's actual legal name, business address, contact details, and applicable privacy responsibilities must be provided in the production version of this policy.

IT TaskFlow is currently a portfolio project, and the production operator has not been established by this document. The product name alone should not be treated as proof of a registered company, legal entity, or appointed data protection officer. Those details must be confirmed rather than replaced with invented business information.

2.2 The role of customer organizations

An organization using the intended platform may decide which employees to onboard, which projects to create, which tasks to assign, which roles to grant, and which work-related records to maintain. Depending on the applicable law, the contract, and the purpose of a particular processing activity, the organization may act as the controller or otherwise bear responsibility for the information it submits and the instructions it gives to the service provider.

For example, a company may decide to record an employee's work email address, job title, project membership, task assignment, task status, or meeting participation. Those decisions are distinct from the technical act of hosting or displaying the information. The organization should determine whether it has an appropriate legal basis, whether the employee has received required notices, and whether the requested use is consistent with employment rules and applicable privacy requirements.

2.3 Responsibility may differ by activity

Responsibility should be assessed for each processing activity instead of assuming that one party controls every type of information. The service operator may determine how its own website security logs are used, while a customer organization may determine the content and recipients of its internal project records. A service provider may process some information only under a customer's documented instructions and may independently handle other information where the law permits or requires it.

Production agreements should document the relevant responsibilities, instructions, confidentiality commitments, security expectations, assistance with privacy requests, and arrangements for returning or deleting information when a service ends. If the applicable legal framework requires a formal data-processing agreement or another contractual document, it should be prepared before the relevant customer data is processed.

03

INFORMATION CATEGORIES

Information we may collect

3.1 Information provided during account registration

If account registration is implemented, the information requested may include a person's name, work email address, account identifier, organization name, job designation, and authentication-related information. Additional details may be requested where they are necessary for a specific function, account recovery, organization administration, or legal obligation. The final registration form should request only information that is reasonably required for the purpose explained to the individual.

An organization administrator may also submit information about a company, department, reporting structure, employee role, or project team. Where an employer creates an employee account, the employee may not personally enter every field. The organization should therefore explain what information it supplies, why the information is needed, and how the employee can correct an inaccurate record.

3.2 Project, task, and collaboration information

The intended platform may contain project names, project descriptions, milestones, task titles, task descriptions, priority levels, due dates, task status, comments, attachments, assignment histories, team membership, and meeting details. Records may also identify the person who created, assigned, updated, reviewed, or completed a task. Such information is often necessary to provide project coordination and accountability features.

Project records can nevertheless contain confidential business information, personal information, source-code references, customer details, or other sensitive content entered by users. Users and organizations should avoid submitting information that is not necessary for their work or that they are not authorized to disclose. The production service should establish suitable rules for permitted attachments, restricted content, access control, and the handling of sensitive material.

3.3 Support requests and communications

If a support function is connected to a working submission service, it may process the name, email address, selected issue category, subject, message, relevant technical details, and files that a person chooses to submit. Follow-up correspondence and the resolution history may also be associated with the request. These records should be used to understand the reported problem, communicate with the requester, and maintain an appropriate support history.

The current frontend portfolio form does not, by itself, establish that a support request is transmitted, stored, or delivered to a support team. That functionality depends on the backend, configured delivery services, and data-handling procedures that are implemented later. Until those components are operational and tested, the page should not promise successful submission or ticket storage.

3.4 Technical, diagnostic, and usage information

A deployed service may process limited technical information such as browser type, operating system, device characteristics, approximate connection information, request timestamps, error messages, security events, and diagnostic logs. Such information may be generated when a browser communicates with a server or when a monitoring component records a service event. Whether these records exist, which fields they contain, and how long they are retained depends on the actual hosting, application, and monitoring configuration.

Usage analytics, event tracking, and performance monitoring should not be assumed to be active simply because they are common in software products. If IT TaskFlow later introduces analytics tools, the policy must identify the relevant categories of data, explain the purposes and applicable choices, and address any consent or notice obligations. Unnecessary collection of browsing or behavioral data should be avoided.

3.5 Information from external sources

If an organization later connects an identity provider, email service, calendar, recruitment system, or other external integration, information may be received from that provider within the permissions granted by the user or organization. The information could include account identifiers, profile details, event information, or records needed for the enabled integration. No external information source should be considered connected unless the integration has actually been implemented and authorized.

Users should review the permissions displayed when authorizing an integration and should understand which information the connected service makes available. Integrations should request only the permissions required for their documented purpose, and any material change to the information obtained through an integration should be reflected in the applicable privacy notices.

04

ORGANIZATION WORKSPACES

Organization and employee data

4.1 Information supplied by an organization

The intended organization workspace may allow authorized representatives to establish a company profile, create departments, configure designations, invite or register employees, create projects, and assign roles. In doing so, the organization may provide information about its business structure and the individuals who participate in its operations. The organization is responsible for ensuring that it is entitled to provide the information and that the information is appropriate for the selected business purpose.

Organization administrators should not enter private information about employees merely because a field is available. Personal identification numbers, financial information, government identifiers, health information, personal contact details, and other sensitive records should not be collected unless a clearly defined feature requires them and the collection is lawful, necessary, and adequately protected. The default design should favor work-related information over unrelated personal information.

4.2 Employee accounts and work records

When an employee uses an organization-managed account, some account and workspace settings may be controlled by the employer or its authorized administrators. Depending on the implemented features, an employer may be able to manage account status, organization membership, project access, task assignments, role permissions, and work-related records. Employees should be informed of these arrangements through appropriate workplace notices and organizational policies.

Work records may include task completion, deadlines, status updates, comments, project participation, and meeting attendance. Such records should be used for the legitimate purposes communicated by the organization, and access should be limited to authorized personnel. The presence of a record in the platform does not, on its own, determine how it may lawfully be used in an employment decision; that question depends on applicable law, company policies, and the circumstances of the decision.

4.3 Project membership and restricted visibility

IT TaskFlow is intended to support role-based and project-specific visibility. Under the proposed design, an employee's Projects menu should display projects for which the employee has a qualifying task assignment or another expressly permitted access grant. Multiple task assignments within the same project should not create duplicate project entries, and access to a project should not automatically grant access to every module or record within it.

The production implementation must enforce access restrictions in the backend, not merely hide menu items in the browser. If an employee's assignment is removed and no other valid access basis remains, the employee's project access should be revoked in accordance with the organization's permissions and applicable retention rules. Records that must be retained for legitimate operational, contractual, or legal purposes may remain available to appropriately authorized personnel even after access changes.

4.4 Organization control and employee requests

Where an organization determines the purpose of employee-data processing, the organization may be the appropriate first contact for requests concerning the accuracy, correction, or use of workplace records. IT TaskFlow should establish a process for identifying whether a request relates to the service operator's own activities or to information processed under a customer's instructions. Requests should then be routed to the appropriate responsible party without unnecessarily exposing the employee's information.

The organization and service operator should cooperate where required by applicable law and their agreements. Neither party should promise that every workplace record can immediately be erased, because certain information may be subject to legal retention, security investigations, dispute preservation, or other lawful restrictions. Any limitation should be assessed individually and explained where legally required.

PURPOSE LIMITATION

How information may be used

5.1 Providing and maintaining the platform

Information may be processed where necessary to provide the functions a user or organization requests. Depending on the released product, these purposes may include creating and maintaining accounts, identifying organization membership, displaying assigned tasks, managing project access, recording status changes, delivering in-platform notifications, and presenting authorized dashboards or reports. Information should be used in a manner consistent with the feature's stated purpose and the permissions associated with the account.

The service may also need to process limited information to troubleshoot errors, maintain availability, investigate reported technical issues, and improve the reliability of implemented features. Such activities should be proportionate to the operational need. Where information can be analyzed without identifying a particular individual, a suitably minimized or aggregated approach should be considered.

5.2 Account administration and communications

Account information may be used to communicate about registration, authentication, account recovery, important configuration changes, security notices, service interruptions, support responses, and other messages necessary for the operation of an account. If a production service sends email or mobile notifications, the actual delivery channels and any associated service providers must be identified and configured before those communications are promised to users.

Optional product announcements, newsletters, or promotional communications should be distinguished from essential account and security messages. Where consent or an unsubscribe mechanism is required, the applicable controls should be implemented and honored. A person should not be required to accept unrelated promotional communications merely to receive an essential account notification unless the relevant arrangement is lawful and properly disclosed.

5.3 Security, misuse prevention, and compliance

Where technically implemented and legally permitted, information may be used to detect unauthorized access, investigate suspicious activity, enforce access permissions, protect the integrity of records, respond to abuse, and comply with valid legal obligations. Security processing may involve relevant authentication events, audit records, request metadata, and details supplied during an incident report. Access to such information should be restricted to authorized people with a legitimate operational or legal need.

Any investigation should be appropriately scoped, documented where necessary, and conducted in accordance with applicable law and organizational agreements. Information collected for security should not automatically be repurposed for unrelated activities simply because it is available. Requests from public authorities should be evaluated for validity, scope, and applicable legal obligations before information is disclosed.

5.4 Product improvement and service analysis

If product analytics or diagnostic tools are introduced, information may be analyzed to understand performance problems, identify confusing workflows, assess feature reliability, and prioritize improvements. Where feasible, product development should rely on aggregated statistics, synthetic examples, or appropriately de-identified records rather than exposing identifiable employee or customer information to people who do not need it.

The service should not claim to collect analytics, record sessions, profile employees, or track behavior unless those activities are actually implemented. If a new purpose materially changes how personal information is used, the relevant privacy notices and legal assessments should be reviewed before the change is introduced.

LEGAL FRAMEWORK

Legal grounds for processing

6.1 Identifying an appropriate legal basis

Where applicable privacy law requires a legal basis for processing personal information, the responsible party must identify and document the basis that applies to each relevant purpose. Depending on the jurisdiction and circumstances, this may involve performing a contract, taking steps requested before entering into a contract, complying with a legal obligation, pursuing a legitimate interest where permitted, or obtaining valid consent where consent is the appropriate basis.

No single legal basis should be assumed to apply to every activity. Processing necessary to administer an account may have a different basis from optional analytics, promotional communications, or an organization's internal employee-management activity. The responsible party should consider the nature of the information, the reasonable expectations of the individual, the purpose of the activity, and any additional conditions imposed by applicable law.

6.2 Consent and withdrawal

Where processing relies on consent, the consent process should provide understandable information about the purpose and should meet the applicable legal requirements for a valid choice. Consent should not be inferred merely because a person visits a website or because an employee has been assigned a work task. Where consent must be freely given, the process should take account of the relationship between the individual and the organization requesting it.

If a person withdraws consent, the responsible party should stop the processing that relied on that consent, subject to applicable law and any other valid basis that may independently permit further processing. Withdrawal does not necessarily make earlier lawful processing invalid, and it does not require deletion of records that must lawfully be retained for a separate reason.

6.3 Legal obligations and jurisdiction

The applicable rules may differ according to the individual's location, the organization's location, the nature of the service, and the location of the responsible entities. The production policy must identify the jurisdictions relevant to the actual service and explain any additional rights, notices, consent requirements, or complaint mechanisms required there.

This portfolio draft is not a determination that IT TaskFlow complies with any particular privacy law. Before commercial launch, the responsible operator should obtain an appropriate legal review, including review of applicable Indian data protection requirements and any other laws relevant to intended customers or users.

IDENTITY AND ACCESS

Account access and permissions

7.1 Account credentials and authentication

Users should keep their account credentials confidential and use only authorized sign-in methods. An actual deployment should implement appropriate authentication controls, account recovery procedures, session handling, and protections against unauthorized access. The precise controls must reflect the risks of the deployed service and the sensitivity of the information it processes.

Passwords, authentication tokens, recovery codes, and other secrets should not be included in support messages or shared through project comments. If a user suspects that an account has been compromised, the user should follow the implemented account-recovery or incident reporting process and notify the appropriate organization administrator where relevant.

7.2 Role-based permissions

The intended platform may provide different permissions for organization administrators, managers, project managers, team members, and employees. Those roles can determine which settings, projects, tasks, employee records, reports, or administrative functions a user may access. Permissions should be based on the person's responsibilities and should not grant broader access merely for convenience.

Role labels and visible navigation items are not sufficient security controls on their own. A production service should verify permissions whenever protected information or actions are requested, including requests made directly to an application endpoint. Authorization checks should prevent users from retrieving another organization's records or viewing project information outside their permitted scope.

7.3 Administrator access and oversight

Authorized administrators may need access to account and organization settings to configure workspaces, manage membership, resolve access problems, and maintain business continuity. Such access should be limited to the permissions required for the assigned responsibilities and should be reviewed when roles change or employment ends. Where appropriate, sensitive administrative actions should be logged and periodically reviewed.

Internal service personnel should not have unrestricted access to customer content merely because they operate the platform. Any support access to customer records should follow documented authorization procedures, be limited to the specific support need, and be subject to confidentiality and security safeguards appropriate to the deployment.

08

BROWSER AND DEVICE DATA

Cookies and technical information

8.1 Essential browser storage

A deployed website may use cookies, local storage, session storage, or similar browser technologies to support functions such as remembering a session, maintaining a selected preference, protecting a workflow, or improving the usability of the interface. The actual technologies used by IT TaskFlow must be verified from its implemented code and hosting configuration.

Browser storage should not be described as secure merely because it is available in the browser. Sensitive authentication secrets should not be placed in storage that exposes them to avoidable script-access risks. Production authentication design should be reviewed against appropriate security practices and the architecture actually selected for the service.

8.2 Analytics and optional technologies

If analytics, advertising, embedded content, or other non-essential technologies are introduced, their purposes and relevant providers should be documented. Where applicable law requires prior consent, those technologies should not be activated before the required consent has been obtained. Users should receive clear information about available controls and the effect of rejecting optional technologies.

A browser setting that blocks cookies may affect some functions, depending on how the service is implemented. The actual consequences should be explained accurately rather than promising that every feature will work identically without browser storage. If the platform does not use a particular category of tracking technology, it should not imply that such tracking is taking place.

8.3 Server logs and diagnostics

Hosting providers and application servers may generate logs containing technical request information, error details, timestamps, or security events. These records may help diagnose failures, maintain service reliability, and investigate abuse. The actual scope of logging depends on the systems selected and the configuration enabled by the service operator.

Logging should be proportionate to operational needs. Sensitive values should be excluded or masked where feasible, access to logs should be restricted, and retention periods should be defined. If a third-party hosting or monitoring provider receives relevant information, that provider's role should be evaluated under the sharing and service-provider provisions of this policy.

09

FUTURE PRODUCT CAPABILITIES

AI and automated features

9.1 Proposed AI-assisted functionality

IT TaskFlow's future roadmap may include AI-assisted task summaries, project insights, interview assistance, automated testing, bug classification, operational recommendations, or workflow automation. These are proposed capabilities, not a statement that every such feature is currently implemented. Before any AI feature is released, its input data, intended output, purpose, limitations, and privacy implications should be assessed and communicated to the people affected.

Depending on the feature, an AI system might process task descriptions, project documentation, code snippets, error messages, candidate responses, or other user-submitted content. Such content may contain confidential business information or personal information. Users should be told what information is necessary for the feature, whether information is sent to another provider, and whether the feature can operate without submitting particular data.

9.2 External AI providers

If an external AI provider is used, the production service should identify the relevant provider and evaluate the provider's processing terms, security arrangements, retention practices, training policies, geographic processing locations, and contractual protections. Information should not be sent to an external AI service without an appropriate technical and legal basis and the required notices, permissions, or agreements.

This policy does not claim that IT TaskFlow currently uses an AI provider, sends user content to an AI model, or operates its own AI infrastructure. Any such arrangement must be verified against the actual implementation and documented before the feature becomes available to users.

9.3 Automated recommendations and decisions

Automated tools may generate suggestions, classifications, summaries, predictions, or scores. Their outputs may be incomplete, inaccurate, or influenced by the limitations of the input data and the underlying technology. Where appropriate, users should be able to review the information and exercise human judgment rather than treating a generated result as automatically correct.

If a future feature materially affects recruitment, employment, access to opportunities, or another important interest, it requires a more specific assessment of applicable law, fairness, transparency, human oversight, and available challenge mechanisms. A general productivity feature should not silently become an employment decision-making system without the required evaluation and disclosures.

9.4 Use of information to improve AI systems

Personal or customer content should not be assumed to be used to train, fine-tune, or improve an AI model simply because the content is processed by an AI-enabled feature. Whether such use occurs depends on the actual system and applicable agreements. Any proposed secondary use should be assessed separately, disclosed clearly, and supported by an appropriate legal basis and required choices.

DISCLOSURE AND PROCESSORS

Sharing and third-party services

10.1 Sharing within an organization

Information entered into an organization workspace may be made available to authorized administrators, managers, project managers, teammates, or other users according to the implemented permissions and the organization's configuration. For example, a task assignment may need to be visible to the assigned employee and relevant project managers, while unrelated employees should not automatically receive access.

Users should consider who can see a record before posting comments, uploading documents, or adding personal information. Organization administrators should configure access based on legitimate business needs and review permissions when employees move between projects or leave the organization.

10.2 Service providers

A production service may rely on third-party providers for hosting, domain management, email delivery, authentication, error monitoring, customer support, file storage, or other operational functions. Where such providers process personal information, the service operator should assess their suitability, document their role, and implement appropriate contractual, technical, and organizational safeguards.

This portfolio document does not establish which providers are currently used by IT TaskFlow. Provider names, categories, processing locations, and relevant privacy links must be added or confirmed once the deployment architecture is selected. A provider should not be described as a processor or independent controller without assessing the actual arrangement.

10.3 Legal requirements and protection of rights

Information may be disclosed where disclosure is required by applicable law or a valid and enforceable legal demand. Where legally permitted, requests should be reviewed for authority, scope, and necessity, and disclosures should be limited to the information lawfully required. The service may also need to preserve or disclose relevant records to establish, exercise, or defend legal claims where permitted.

10.4 Business transfers

If the service operator undergoes a merger, acquisition, restructuring, financing, or transfer of relevant assets, information may need to be reviewed or transferred as part of the transaction, subject to applicable law and appropriate safeguards. Any recipient should be required to handle the information consistently with applicable legal requirements and the commitments that continue to apply.

10.5 No unrestricted sale of personal information

The intended privacy approach is not to sell personal information as a standalone commercial product. Information should not be disclosed to unrelated third parties for their independent marketing without an appropriate legal basis and the notices or choices required by law. The final production policy must accurately describe any actual advertising, data-sharing, or monetization arrangements rather than relying on a broad promise that has not been verified.

DATA LOCATION

International data transfers

11.1 Processing locations

Depending on the hosting environment, infrastructure providers, support arrangements, and integrations selected for a production deployment, information may be processed or accessed in more than one country. The location of the user does not by itself establish the physical location of every system that handles information. The service operator should identify relevant locations and transfer arrangements before launch.

11.2 Safeguards for transfers

Where a transfer of personal information is subject to specific legal conditions, the responsible party should use a lawful transfer mechanism and implement any required contractual, organizational, or technical safeguards. These may vary according to the applicable jurisdiction, the destination, the recipient, and the type of information involved.

IT TaskFlow should not promise that all information remains in a particular country unless the production architecture and provider agreements substantiate that commitment. If data residency options are offered in the future, their scope, limitations, and exceptions should be explained separately.

12

SECURITY AND SAFEGUARDS

Information security

12.1 A risk-based security approach

Information security should be designed around the nature of the information being handled, the possible consequences of unauthorized access, and the architecture of the service. Appropriate measures may include access controls, authentication protections, secure development practices, vulnerability management, backups, logging, monitoring, incident response, and staff confidentiality obligations. The specific measures implemented must be verified before being represented as active safeguards.

12.2 Protection during transmission and storage

A production deployment should assess how information is protected when transmitted between a user's browser and the service, between internal components, and while stored by the relevant systems. Encryption, key management, credential storage, backup protection, and access restrictions should be implemented according to the service's risks and technical requirements.

This document does not claim that any particular encryption standard, storage configuration, backup process, or security certification has already been implemented. Such statements should only be made after technical verification and, where relevant, independent assessment.

12.3 Organizational access and confidentiality

People who are authorized to handle personal or customer information should receive appropriate instructions and access only to information needed for their responsibilities. Administrative access should be reviewed periodically, particularly when duties change or access is no longer necessary. Where appropriate, privileged operations should be logged and reviewed.

12.4 User and organization responsibilities

Security is a shared responsibility. Organizations should appoint appropriate administrators, manage access grants, promptly remove permissions that are no longer required, and communicate relevant workplace rules. Users should protect credentials, use supported devices and browsers, avoid sharing confidential content with unauthorized people, and report suspected incidents through the designated channel.

12.5 Limits of security assurances

No internet-connected system can be guaranteed to be entirely free from risk. A production service should implement reasonable safeguards, monitor relevant risks, and respond to identified weaknesses. This statement does not replace a documented security program or authorize the service operator to make unsupported claims about security performance.

13

LIFECYCLE MANAGEMENT

Retention and deletion

13.1 Retaining information only as needed

Personal information should be retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it is processed, subject to applicable legal, contractual, security, and operational requirements. Retention periods may differ for account records, project content, support requests, diagnostic logs, backups, and records that must be preserved for a lawful reason.

The production service should establish retention schedules for the information categories it actually collects. It should not retain identifiable information indefinitely merely because storage is available. Where a defined retention period is not feasible, the reason and the criteria used to determine retention should be documented and explained where required by law.

13.2 Closing an account or organization workspace

If account closure or organization termination is implemented, the service should define how account information, organization membership, task records, attachments, and related content are handled. Depending on the customer's configuration and applicable agreements, an organization may be offered a reasonable opportunity to export records before the workspace is closed. Such export capabilities should not be promised until they are implemented and tested.

13.3 Deletion and residual copies

When information is eligible for deletion, the responsible party should follow a documented process appropriate to the systems in which it is held. Certain records may need to be retained where required by law, necessary for a valid legal claim, or otherwise lawfully preserved. Any applicable exception should be assessed rather than applied automatically to every request.

Deleted records may remain temporarily in protected backups or disaster recovery systems until those copies expire under the documented backup lifecycle. Where this occurs, the service should prevent the information from being restored to ordinary active use except as necessary for legitimate recovery purposes and subject to appropriate controls.

13.4 Retention by customer organizations

Organizations may maintain their own records outside IT TaskFlow, including copies in internal systems, exported reports, emails, or independently managed backups. Closing an account or deleting a record from the platform does not necessarily delete every separate copy held by an organization or another recipient. Requests concerning those copies may need to be directed to the party that controls them.

14

INDIVIDUAL CHOICES

Privacy rights and choices

14.1 Rights under applicable law

Depending on the applicable legal framework, an individual may have rights concerning access to personal information, correction of inaccurate details, deletion, withdrawal of consent, objection to certain processing, data portability, restriction of processing, or complaint to a relevant authority. The exact rights, conditions, exceptions, and response periods vary by jurisdiction and circumstance.

This policy does not assert that every listed right applies to every user or every type of record. The responsible party should determine which rights apply and provide a workable process for exercising them. Requests should be handled in accordance with applicable law, including any lawful exceptions or identity verification requirements.

14.2 How to submit a privacy request

A production version of IT TaskFlow should provide a designated privacy contact or request process. Individuals should describe the request clearly, identify the account or organization involved where relevant, and provide only the information reasonably needed to locate the record. Sensitive identity documents should not be sent through ordinary support channels unless an appropriate secure procedure has been established.

The responsible party may need to verify the requester's identity or authority before disclosing, changing, or deleting information. Verification should be proportionate to the sensitivity of the requested action. Requests made by an employee about organization-controlled records may need coordination with the employer, depending on the legal and contractual responsibilities involved.

14.3 Account details and corrections

Users should be able to identify an appropriate route for correcting inaccurate account information. Where profile editing is supported, the user may be able to update certain fields directly. Other fields, such as organization membership or employer-managed details, may require an authorized administrator to make the change. Corrections should be handled without unnecessarily disclosing other people's information.

14.4 Complaints and escalation

If an individual believes that their information has been handled improperly, they should be able to raise the concern through the designated privacy contact or applicable complaint mechanism. The production operator should define how complaints are acknowledged, assessed, investigated, documented, and resolved. Where the law provides a right to complain to a regulator or other competent authority, that right remains subject to the relevant legal requirements.

15

AGE AND ELIGIBILITY

Children's information

15.1 Intended audience

IT TaskFlow is being designed primarily as a workplace productivity and organization collaboration platform. Its intended audience includes organizations and people participating in professional or work-related activities. The final service should establish appropriate account eligibility requirements that reflect the product's purpose and applicable law.

15.2 Information relating to minors

The service should not knowingly collect or process children's personal information in a manner that violates applicable legal requirements. Where a lawful business use could involve a minor, such as an authorized training or educational arrangement, the responsible organization and service operator must assess the relevant age-related requirements, notices, consent rules, and safeguards before processing the information.

If the operator becomes aware that personal information has been collected in violation of applicable requirements, it should assess the situation and take appropriate steps, which may include restricting processing, contacting the responsible organization, or deleting information where legally appropriate. Requests should be handled in accordance with applicable law and any required verification process.

External websites and links

16.1 Links to other websites

IT TaskFlow pages may link to external websites, documentation, support resources, service providers, or other online destinations. Once a user follows an external link, the destination website's own privacy practices may apply. The presence of a link does not mean that IT TaskFlow controls that website or endorses every aspect of its data-handling practices.

16.2 Integrations and embedded content

If embedded videos, external forms, analytics scripts, maps, or other third-party components are added to the service, they may process technical information when loaded or used. The production operator should assess each component, document relevant data flows, and implement required disclosures or consent controls. External components should not be embedded without considering their privacy and security implications.

Users should review the privacy information supplied by external providers before connecting accounts or submitting information to them. IT TaskFlow should explain any permissions requested by an integration and provide a suitable method to disconnect it where the feature supports disconnection.

INCIDENT RESPONSE

Data incidents and notifications

17.1 Assessing a suspected incident

A data incident may involve unauthorized access, accidental disclosure, loss, alteration, unavailability, or another event that affects the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information. The production operator should maintain an incident response procedure proportionate to the service, including a method to report suspected incidents, assess their scope, contain the problem, and determine appropriate corrective action.

17.2 Communication and legal notifications

Where an incident triggers a legal notification duty, the responsible party should notify the [L18] appropriate authority, affected organization, or individual within the applicable period and in accordance with the governing requirements. The content and timing of a notification should reflect verified information about the incident and the risks to affected people.

The operator should not promise that every incident will be detected immediately or that every event will have no impact. Instead, it should establish appropriate detection, escalation, investigation, and communication procedures. Customer agreements should define relevant responsibilities and cooperation requirements where appropriate.

INTERPRETATION

Definitions and interpretation

20.1 Personal information

In this document, personal information generally refers to information that identifies a person, relates to an identifiable person, or is otherwise treated as personal data under the applicable legal framework. The precise definition may differ by jurisdiction. Work email addresses, account identifiers, assignment histories, and other work-related records may qualify as personal information when they relate to an identifiable individual.

20.2 Processing

Processing generally includes operations performed on information, whether by automated means or otherwise. Depending on the context, it may include collecting, recording, organizing, storing, retrieving, viewing, using, sharing, correcting, restricting, or deleting information. A specific legal framework may provide its own definition and requirements.

20.3 Organization, user, and service provider

An organization is a business or other entity that uses or intends to use the platform for its authorized activities. A user is an individual who visits, accesses, or uses the website or a platform feature. A service provider is a separate entity engaged to supply a technical or operational service. These labels are descriptive and do not by themselves determine a party's legal role under applicable law.

20.4 Reading this policy with other documents

This Privacy Policy should be read together with applicable terms of service, customer agreements, feature-specific notices, and other legal documents relevant to the actual service. If a conflict arises, the parties should identify the applicable contractual and legal requirements rather than assuming that one generic provision resolves every issue. Nothing in this portfolio draft is intended to remove a right that cannot lawfully be excluded or restricted.
