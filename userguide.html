START HERE5 min read

Getting started with IT TaskFlow

Build confidence with the basics before you dive into your daily work.

IT TaskFlow is designed to help people organize work in one place. Depending on your assigned role and access permissions, you may be able to view tasks, participate in projects, collaborate with teammates, and track progress from your workspace.

Start by signing in with the account details provided to you. Take a moment to explore the navigation, your profile, and the areas available to your role. Your workspace may look different from another employee's workspace because access can depend on responsibilities and permissions.

Once you understand where your main tools are located, choose one simple activity to practise, such as reviewing a task or checking its due date. Learning the interface gradually makes it easier to use the platform confidently without missing important work.

Your first steps
  1. Open the application and sign in.
  2. Review your profile and available navigation.
  3. Open a task or project you have access to.
  4. Check the task status, owner, and due date.
✦
Helpful tip

Only use the account assigned to you. If a page or feature is missing, ask your organization administrator to check your access.

Understanding your workspace

Know where to find your work, profile settings, and the tools available to you.

Your workspace is the starting point for the activities available to your account. Depending on the current application configuration, it may provide shortcuts to tasks, projects, notifications, calendars, reports, or team-related information.

Use the main navigation to move between available areas. Some sections may only be visible to managers or administrators, so a missing menu item does not necessarily mean something is broken. Your role and your organization's setup determine which tools you can access.

Keep your profile details accurate and review notifications regularly. A quick check at the beginning of your workday can help you identify new assignments, approaching deadlines, and updates that require your attention.

Get familiar with your workspace
  1. Locate the main navigation menu.
  2. Open your profile and review its details.
  3. Check your assigned tasks and notifications.
  4. Bookmark or remember the areas you use most.

DAILY WORK6 min read

Managing tasks from start to finish

Learn how tasks are organized, assigned, updated, and completed.

A task represents a specific piece of work that needs an outcome. A useful task description explains what needs to be done, why it matters, and what completion should look like. Clear details reduce confusion and make it easier for the assigned person to begin.

When creating or assigning a task, provide an informative title, relevant instructions, an appropriate priority, and a realistic due date where those fields are available. Assign the task to the correct team member and verify that the person has the necessary context and access before work begins.

As work progresses, update the task status to reflect its real condition. Add comments when a decision, blocker, or handover needs to be recorded. Before marking a task complete, verify that the expected work is finished and communicate any remaining follow-up actions.

Recommended task workflow
  1. Create a task with a clear title and description.
  2. Assign the responsible team member.
  3. Set priority and due date when applicable.
  4. Track progress and record important updates.
  5. Confirm completion and close the task.
✦
Helpful tip

Use one task for one clear outcome. Break large pieces of work into smaller tasks that can be tracked independently.

TEAMWORK5 min read

Projects and team collaboration

Connect related work and make team responsibilities easier to understand.

A project groups related activities around a shared goal. Instead of treating every task as unrelated work, a project helps team members understand how individual responsibilities contribute to a larger outcome.

Project visibility and editing permissions may depend on the role assigned by your organization. Project leads and authorized managers can coordinate responsibilities, while team members focus on the tasks available to them. Check project details before creating duplicate work or changing shared information.

Good collaboration depends on timely updates and clear communication. Record decisions in the appropriate task or project discussion, flag dependencies early, and tell the relevant person when a deadline or requirement changes. This creates a useful history for teammates who need to understand the current status.

Work effectively with your team
  1. Open the project you are authorized to access.
  2. Review its goals and related tasks.
  3. Confirm owners and responsibilities.
  4. Share progress and raise blockers early.
✦
Helpful tip

Keep project updates factual and concise. Explain what changed, what is blocked, and what action is needed next.

STAY ON TRACK4 min read

Calendar, deadlines, and notifications

Keep upcoming responsibilities visible and avoid missing important updates.

Due dates and calendar views help you understand when work needs attention. When a calendar is available, use it to review upcoming deadlines and identify days when several responsibilities may overlap.

Notifications can draw your attention to new assignments, comments, status changes, or other activity supported by your application. Review each notification and open the related task or project to understand the context before taking action.

Notifications are useful reminders, but they should not replace your own planning. Check important deadlines directly, keep task dates current, and contact the task owner if a date appears incorrect. Available notification options may vary depending on the features enabled for your account.

Build a reliable routine
  1. Review upcoming due dates at the start of your day.
  2. Open new notifications and read their context.
  3. Update dates when an approved change occurs.
  4. Review your next day's priorities before signing off.
✦
Helpful tip

When a deadline changes, communicate the reason and update the relevant task if you have permission to do so.

INSIGHTS5 min read

Reports and progress tracking

Understand progress indicators and use available reporting tools responsibly.

Reports and analytics, when enabled, help authorized users understand the state of work across tasks or projects. Depending on the available features, reports may summarize completion, outstanding work, deadlines, or other progress indicators.

Read each metric in context. A high number of completed tasks does not necessarily mean a project is on schedule, and a list of overdue items may need to be considered alongside dependencies or recently changed requirements. Confirm the reporting period and filters before drawing conclusions.

Use reporting information to identify patterns and decide where follow-up is needed. Managers may use the available information to coordinate workload and resolve blockers, while team members can use their own task information to plan the next steps. Access to reports may be restricted by role.

Read reports with confidence
  1. Open a report available to your role.
  2. Check its date range and filters.
  3. Review completed, active, and delayed work.
  4. Confirm unusual results before acting on them.
✦
Helpful tip

Treat metrics as a starting point for investigation, not the full explanation of a team's performance.

ACCOUNT CARE4 min read

Account settings and security

Protect your account and understand how access permissions work.

Your account provides access to work information and features assigned to you. Keep your profile information accurate and use only the account provided for your role. If you believe your account details are incorrect, contact the appropriate administrator instead of creating an unapproved duplicate account.

Use a strong, unique password and never share it with teammates. Sign out when using a shared device, avoid leaving sensitive work visible to others, and follow your organization's security policies. If you suspect unauthorized access, report it promptly through the approved support channel.

Access permissions help ensure people can view and change only the information appropriate to their responsibilities. If you need a feature for legitimate work, request access from your manager or administrator and explain why it is required. Do not try to bypass access controls or use another person's credentials.

Keep your account protected
  1. Use a strong, unique password.
  2. Keep your login details private.
  3. Sign out on shared or public devices.
  4. Report suspicious activity promptly.
✦
Helpful tip

If your organization supports multi-factor authentication, enable and use it according to your administrator's instructions.

ACCESS & GOVERNANCE

Roles, Permissions & Administration

Understand the responsibilities of employees, team leads, project managers, HR teams, and organization administrators. Learn how organization-level access differs from the internal IT TaskFlow Business Admin Panel.

!

Two separate administration levels

Organization Administration manages an individual customer's workspace, employees, projects, and permitted settings. The IT TaskFlow Business Admin Panel is reserved for authorized internal platform administrators who manage the IT TaskFlow product itself.

Understand the organization roles

Each role should receive only the access needed to perform its responsibilities. Exact permissions depend on the organization's configured roles and the functionality implemented in the application.

01 Individual contributor

Employee / Team Member

Works on assigned tasks and contributes to authorized projects.

Typical responsibilities
  • View assigned tasks and permitted project information.
  • Update task progress and report blockers.
  • Participate in authorized comments and team discussions.
  • Review relevant notifications and due dates.
Access boundary

Should not automatically see every project or another employee's private or restricted information.

02 Team coordination

Team Lead

Coordinates the work of a team and helps maintain delivery quality.

Typical responsibilities
  • Review team task progress and identify blockers.
  • Coordinate priorities and work dependencies.
  • Support employees with task-related questions.
  • Escalate delays and delivery risks to the project manager.
Access boundary

Team visibility and task-assignment authority should be limited to the teams and projects the lead is authorized to manage.

03 Project delivery

Project Manager

Plans and oversees delivery for the projects assigned to them.

Typical responsibilities
  • Manage authorized project workspaces and priorities.
  • Create tasks and assign work within permitted scope.
  • Monitor deadlines, progress, risks, and dependencies.
  • Review project reports and coordinate team delivery.
Access boundary

Project management access should not automatically grant organization-wide administration or internal platform access.

04 People operations

HR Team / HR Manager

Supports employee lifecycle activities within the HR team's authorized responsibilities.

Typical responsibilities
  • Maintain permitted employee and designation information.
  • Coordinate onboarding and employee record updates.
  • Work with authorized managers on team information.
  • Support HR workflows where those modules are available.
Access boundary

HR access should be scoped to approved people-related data. HR roles should not automatically receive access to all project, financial, or platform administration settings.

05 Organization workspace

Organization Administrator

Administers the customer's IT TaskFlow organization within the permissions granted to that account.

Typical responsibilities
  • Manage permitted organization and workspace settings.
  • Coordinate employee onboarding and account access.
  • Assign roles where authorized by the permission model.
  • Review organization-level activity and configuration.
Access boundary

Organization administration applies to that customer's workspace, not the internal IT TaskFlow platform or other customer organizations.

06 Internal platform only

IT TaskFlow Business Admin

Manages the IT TaskFlow product and its platform-level configuration from a separate internal administration area.

Intended responsibilities
  • Configure platform-wide settings and permission policies.
  • Oversee customer organization onboarding.
  • Manage platform role templates and available designations.
  • Administer subscription settings and product configuration.
  • Review authorized platform-level operational information.
Restricted access

This panel is for explicitly authorized internal administrators. It must not be exposed as a normal customer dashboard or granted to a customer manager simply because they manage a project.

STRUCTURE 05

How to organize teams in IT TaskFlow

Use this suggested setup sequence when configuring an organization's structure. Available controls depend on the implementation.

  1. 1

    Define the departments

    Identify the functional departments used by the organization, such as Engineering, QA, HR, and DevOps.

  2. 2

    Define teams and team leads

    Establish team membership and designate the appropriate team lead or functional manager.

  3. 3

    Associate employee records

    Assign each employee to the correct department, team, designation, and reporting manager.

  4. 4

    Configure project teams separately

    Add authorized employees to their project workspaces according to project requirements.

  5. 5

    Verify visibility and permissions

    Check that employees, team leads, and project managers can access the information required for their responsibilities, without exposing unrelated workspaces.

Common team structure issues

Review the relevant relationships and permissions before escalating a configuration issue.

An employee belongs to the wrong department
  • Verify the employee's current department and team.
  • Confirm the intended change with the responsible manager or HR team.
  • Ask an authorized administrator to update the organization record.
  • Check whether any dependent team assignments need review.
The employee reports to the wrong manager
  • Confirm the correct reporting relationship with the organization.
  • Distinguish the functional manager from the project manager.
  • Update the appropriate reporting field through the supported process.
  • Verify that any related approval or reporting workflows remain correct.
A project manager cannot see the correct project team
  • Verify that the correct project workspace is selected.
  • Check project membership and the manager's project permissions.
  • Confirm that team members were added to the project separately.
  • Ask an authorized administrator to review the configuration if needed.
A team member can see unrelated projects
  • Review the employee's project memberships and assigned roles.
  • Check whether organization-wide access was granted unintentionally.
  • Correct excessive permissions through the authorized process.
  • Verify access restrictions for the affected projects.
✓

Best practices for team and reporting management

  • Keep department membership separate from project membership.
  • Distinguish functional reporting managers from project managers.
  • Assign one clear primary reporting manager where company policy requires it.
  • Allow cross-functional project teams without unnecessarily changing department membership.
  • Review team and project access when employees move between roles or projects.
  • Restrict organizational charts and employee details according to their sensitivity.

Feature availability: The hierarchy and examples on this page are illustrative. Department management, reporting relationships, team membership, and project-specific access must be implemented and tested in IT TaskFlow before they can be treated as live features.
