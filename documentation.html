OVERVIEW

What is IT TaskFlow?

IT TaskFlow is a work and project management platform designed to connect organizations, projects, teams, employees, and tasks within a structured workflow.

It provides a centralized environment where work can be planned, created, assigned, executed, monitored, and completed while keeping project and team activities connected.

IT TaskFlow is designed to support different levels of an organization. Employees can manage their assigned work, Team Leads can coordinate team activities, Managers can monitor projects and progress, and Administrators can manage organization-level settings, users, roles, and permissions.

In simple terms

IT TaskFlow connects people with the work they are responsible for and provides visibility into how that work moves from planning to completion.

PURPOSE

Why IT TaskFlow?

Modern software and business teams often work across multiple projects, people, deadlines, and activities. IT TaskFlow provides a structured way to organize this work and make responsibilities and progress easier to understand.

01

Centralized Work

Keep projects, tasks, teams, employees, activities, and related work information connected within one platform.

02

Clear Ownership

Assign work to the appropriate employees or team members so responsibilities are easier to identify and track.

03

Better Visibility

Provide teams and managers with visibility into task progress, project activity, deadlines, and outstanding work.

04

Connected Workflow

Connect planning, task execution, collaboration, monitoring, completion, and reporting into a structured workflow.

PROBLEM SPACE

Problems IT TaskFlow Addresses

Work becomes difficult to manage when information, responsibilities, communication, and progress are spread across disconnected processes or tools. IT TaskFlow is structured around these common workflow challenges.

01

Scattered Work Information

Project information, tasks, deadlines, discussions, and activities can become difficult to track when they are distributed across different locations or tools.

02

Unclear Ownership

Teams need a clear way to identify who is responsible for specific tasks and what work each person is expected to complete.

03

Limited Project Visibility

Team Leads and Managers need visibility into project progress, pending work, deadlines, completion, and team activity.

04

Disconnected Workflows

Requirements, planning, development, testing, deployment, and support can involve multiple activities and teams. A structured workflow helps connect these activities.

Getting Started

Learn how to access IT TaskFlow, configure your account, navigate the application, and begin working with tasks and projects.

01. Before You Begin

Make sure you have access to the application, a supported browser, and a registered or organization-provided account. Available features depend on your role and permissions.

02. Create Your Account

  1. Open the IT TaskFlow application.
  2. Select the available sign-up option.
  3. Enter the requested account information.
  4. Complete any verification steps if configured.
  5. Continue to the next step shown by the application.

If your organization manages employee accounts, follow the login instructions provided by your administrator.

03. Sign In

  1. Open the sign-in page.
  2. Enter your registered credentials.
  3. Submit the sign-in form.
  4. Complete additional verification if enabled.
  5. Confirm that you can access the appropriate account.

04. Complete Your First-Time Setup

  • Review your profile information.
  • Understand your assigned role and permissions.
  • Confirm your organization, team, or project access.
  • Review the available notification settings.

05. Understand the Application Navigation

  • Tasks: Review assigned work and task progress.
  • Projects: Access projects available to your account.
  • Team Collaboration: Review team activity and work updates.
  • Calendar: Review supported deadlines and schedules.
  • Reports & Analytics: View available progress summaries.
  • Notifications: Review relevant application updates.

06. Find Your First Task

  1. Open the task list or project available to you.
  2. Find a task assigned to your account.
  3. Read its title, description, priority, and due date.
  4. Review the completion criteria and related information.
  5. Ask the task owner for clarification if needed.

07. Update Your Task Progress

  1. Review the task requirements.
  2. Start working according to the configured workflow.
  3. Update progress and communicate blockers.
  4. Provide relevant comments or attachments if supported.
  5. Complete the task according to its requirements.

Task statuses and permitted transitions may vary according to the project's configured workflow and your permissions.

08. Understand Notifications

Depending on the features available, notifications may inform you about task assignments, status updates, comments, mentions, deadlines, and project activity.

09. Troubleshoot Common Issues

  • Cannot sign in: Check your credentials and account status.
  • Cannot see a project: Verify project membership and permissions.
  • Cannot update a task: Check your editing permissions and workflow rules.
  • Feature unavailable: It may not be implemented or enabled for your account.

10. Your First-Day Checklist

  1. Create or receive your account.
  2. Sign in and review your profile.
  3. Confirm your role and project access.
  4. Find your assigned work.
  5. Update progress and complete a task.

09 WORK ITEMS

Milestone

Learn more

10 PROCESSES

Backlog and Sprint

A backlog organizes planned work. A sprint is a time-boxed period for completing selected work.

In Scrum-based teams, the backlog helps prioritize upcoming work, while sprint planning selects work for the sprint. These concepts apply when an Agile workflow is configured or supported.

11 PROCESSES

Learn more

Priority examples include Low, Medium, High, and Urgent. Status examples include To Do, In Progress, In Review, and Done. Actual options depend on the configured workflow.

12 PROCESSES

Learn more

A workflow can define permitted status transitions, review steps, and completion rules. Assignment and status changes should follow the user's access permissions.

13 COLLABORATION

Activity, Comments and Attachments

Supporting information that helps teams communicate, share context, and track work.

Learn more

Comments provide discussion, attachments supply relevant files, and activity history can record important changes. Availability depends on the implemented module.

14 COLLABORATION

Learn more

Depending on implementation, notifications may cover assignments, comments, mentions, due dates, and status changes. Delivery channels and preferences may vary.

15 INSIGHTS

Summaries that help teams understand workload, task progress, and project delivery.

Learn more

Possible measures include completed tasks, overdue work, workload distribution, and project progress. Available reports depend on the data and features implemented.

COMMON QUESTIONS

QA troubleshooting guide

What should I do when a test fails?

Confirm the test steps, environment, input data, and expected result. Reproduce the issue, capture evidence, and report it if the failure is genuine. Record environment or test-data problems separately.

What is the difference between severity and priority?

Severity describes the impact of a defect on the system or users. Priority describes how urgently the team should address it. They are related but not interchangeable.

Why can a defect reappear after a fix?

The fix may be incomplete, the environment may differ, or a later change may have introduced a regression. Reproduce the issue, collect evidence, and share the findings with the development team.

When should regression testing be performed?

Perform relevant regression tests after changes that could affect existing behavior, including bug fixes, feature updates, configuration changes, and selected release candidates.

Does passing all test cases guarantee a defect-free release?

No. Testing reduces risk but cannot prove that software has no defects. Coverage gaps, untested conditions, environment differences, and new usage patterns can still reveal issues.

COMMON QUESTIONS

DevOps troubleshooting guide

Why might a CI pipeline fail?

Possible causes include failing tests, invalid configuration, missing dependencies, permission errors, or incorrect workflow paths. Start with the first failing step in the pipeline logs.

What is the difference between CI and CD?

Continuous Integration validates code changes frequently. Continuous Delivery prepares validated changes for release, while Continuous Deployment automatically releases qualifying changes to production.

Why does a deployment work in testing but fail in production?

Environment variables, permissions, configuration, data, infrastructure, or dependency versions may differ. Compare the environments and inspect deployment and application logs.

How should secrets be stored?

Use an appropriate secret manager or protected platform secrets. Avoid committing passwords, access tokens, private keys, or other credentials to source control.

When should a release be rolled back?

Consider rollback when a release causes a critical regression or violates agreed health criteria and recovery by a forward fix is not the safer immediate option. Follow the team's incident procedure and verify the recovery.

API troubleshooting

The API returns 400 Bad Request

Inspect the request body, required fields, parameter names, data types, and validation rules. Check the API's documented error response for the specific cause.

Check whether authentication is required and whether the session or token is present, valid, and unexpired. Never paste secrets into public logs or screenshots.

COMMON ISSUES

Integration troubleshooting

Use these checks when a connected service does not behave as expected.

An external API returns an error

Inspect the HTTP status, safe error details, endpoint configuration, authentication, permissions, and request schema. Check the provider's current API documentation and avoid repeatedly retrying permanent client errors.

A webhook event is not received

Verify the configured endpoint, network reachability, event subscription, and delivery logs. Confirm that the receiving service accepts the expected method and payload and validates the webhook signature.

The same event is processed more than once

Use a stable event identifier and idempotent processing. Record successfully handled events and make retries safe so duplicate delivery does not create duplicate tasks or notifications.

An integration stops working after a credential change

Verify credential validity, expiration, granted scopes, and the active configuration. Rotate credentials through a controlled process and never expose tokens in browser code or logs.

Updates are delayed or missing

Review provider rate limits, queue or retry status, timeouts, synchronization rules, and timestamp handling. Use correlation IDs to trace a single event across the integration flow.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Troubleshooting FAQs

Quick answers for common development and deployment questions.

Where should I start when a page is completely blank?

Confirm the page URL and HTML file first. Then inspect the Console and Network panels for JavaScript errors or missing resources. Fix the first relevant error and reload.

Why does the page work locally but fail on GitHub Pages?

Check publishing settings, workflow results, relative file paths, filename capitalization, and the published output. A path that works on one local environment may fail on a case-sensitive host.

How can I debug a button that does nothing?

Check the Console, verify the button selector, and confirm that the event listener is attached after the relevant element exists. Also inspect whether another script error stopped execution.

Should I rewrite a whole file to fix one small issue?

Usually not. Reproduce the defect, identify the smallest affected area, review the diff, and test the original behavior again. Keep a working Git checkpoint before significant changes.

What should I check if task data is missing?

Trace the selected task from its source through assignment, filtering, and rendering. If a backend is introduced later, inspect the request status and response without exposing credentials or private records.

01 What is IT TaskFlow?

IT TaskFlow is a task and work-management application concept designed to organize tasks, support team coordination, and document software-development workflows in one place.

The intended users include individual contributors, QA professionals, developers, team leads, project managers, and organizations that need structured work coordination. Available access depends on the functionality enabled in the current version.

04 What is the difference between a task and a project?

A task is an individual piece of work with its own status, owner, and due date where applicable. A project groups related tasks and activities toward a larger objective.

Task assignment is part of the planned work-management experience. Assignment options and access rules depend on the current implementation and the user's role.

Continuous Integration (CI) frequently integrates code changes and validates them with automated checks. Continuous Delivery keeps software ready for release; Continuous Deployment goes further by automatically deploying qualifying changes.

The current frontend is being built with HTML5, CSS3, and vanilla JavaScript. The existing project structure is being extended incrementally without introducing a frontend framework.

Role-based access is a design consideration for IT TaskFlow. In a production system, the backend must verify permissions for protected actions and data; hiding a button in the frontend is not sufficient security.

15 Will two-factor authentication be available?

Two-factor authentication can be added as part of the account security design. It should only be presented as available to users after the authentication flow, recovery process, and server-side verification have been implemented and tested.
