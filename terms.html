IT TASKFLOW · TERMS OF USE

Agreement governing the use of IT TaskFlow

Please read this document carefully. It describes the proposed rules, responsibilities and limitations that apply when an organization or an individual accesses or uses IT TaskFlow.

01

FOUNDATION OF THE AGREEMENT

Introduction and Acceptance of Terms

1.1 Introduction to IT TaskFlow

IT TaskFlow is a software platform concept designed to help organizations coordinate technology teams, manage projects, assign and track tasks, organize work activities, maintain project-related records and support collaboration among authorized users. Depending on the version and features made available, the platform may include organizational workspaces, employee onboarding workflows, project dashboards, notifications, meeting coordination, testing workflows, bug tracking, reporting and other productivity tools. The features available to a user may depend on the product version, configuration, role, permissions and any applicable service arrangement.

1.2 Agreement to these Terms

These Terms and Conditions describe the proposed contractual framework for accessing and using IT TaskFlow. They apply, as appropriate, to organizations that establish workspaces, authorized representatives who administer those workspaces, employees or contractors invited to participate, and other individuals who access the platform with permission. Where these terms are formally adopted for a live service, access or use will be subject to the acceptance process presented at registration, onboarding or another appropriate point. Mere presentation of this portfolio draft does not itself establish a binding customer agreement.

1.3 Organization and individual users

An organization may establish internal rules for the use of IT TaskFlow by its personnel. Those internal rules may address project procedures, reporting requirements, confidentiality, employee conduct, information classification and work approvals. Such rules should remain consistent with applicable law and any binding agreement governing the service. An organization cannot authorize an individual to access information beyond the authority the organization is entitled to grant, nor can an individual use an organizational account to bypass applicable access restrictions.

1.4 Related policies and additional agreements

These terms are intended to operate alongside a separate Privacy Policy and, where relevant, additional order forms, service descriptions, organization agreements, security terms or other documents expressly incorporated into a finalized agreement. If different documents address the same subject inconsistently, the applicable precedence should be specified in the relevant signed or accepted agreement. A policy or document will not automatically become a binding contractual term merely because it is linked from a website.

1.5 Changes to the service or agreement

Software platforms evolve as features are added, revised, restricted or retired. The service description and applicable terms may therefore need to change as IT TaskFlow develops. For a live service, material contractual changes should be communicated through an appropriate notice and handled in accordance with applicable law and any required consent process. Continuing access should not be treated as acceptance where applicable law requires a different form of agreement.

MEANING OF IMPORTANT TERMS

Definitions and Interpretation

2.1 Defined terms

For purposes of this portfolio draft, “Platform” means the IT TaskFlow website, application interfaces and associated features that are actually made available for use. “Organization” means a company, business, institution or other entity that establishes or controls an organizational workspace. “Organization Administrator” means a person authorized to manage organization-level settings and access. “User” means an individual who accesses the Platform, including an administrator, manager, project manager, employee, contractor or other authorized participant.

“Workspace” means an organizational environment within which permitted users may access relevant records and tools. “Project” means a defined collection of work, tasks, participants and associated project information. “Task” means an individual work item or activity recorded through an available task-management feature. “Organization Content” means information, documents, records, files or other material submitted to or created within the Platform by or for an organization and its users.

“Personal Information” means information relating to an identified or identifiable individual, as defined under applicable data-protection law. “Third-Party Service” means a product, platform, website, infrastructure provider or integration operated by an entity other than the operator of IT TaskFlow. “Documentation” means user guides, technical explanations, instructions and other official materials published for the Platform. These definitions may be refined in a finalized legal agreement.

2.2 Interpretation of the agreement

References to a person may include an individual or, where appropriate, a legal entity. Words such as “including” are intended to introduce examples rather than an exhaustive list, unless the context expressly requires otherwise. References to a law include amendments and replacement legislation to the extent applicable. Headings are provided for readability and do not independently expand or restrict the meaning of a substantive provision.

2.3 Product descriptions and examples

Examples of features, workflows, roles or integrations are explanatory unless the finalized service agreement expressly commits to providing them. Screenshots, demonstrations, roadmap statements and sample records should not be interpreted as guarantees that a feature is production-ready, available to every customer or suitable for a particular business purpose.

ELIGIBILITY AND AUTHORIZATION

Eligibility and Authority to Enter into an Agreement

3.1 Authority of organizations

A person creating or administering an organization workspace must have the authority required to act for that organization. By registering on behalf of an entity under a finalized agreement, the representative would be expected to confirm that the entity has authorized the registration and that the representative may accept applicable contractual terms. A person must not impersonate an organization, falsely claim authority or register an entity using information that the person is not entitled to provide.

3.2 Eligibility of individual users

Individual users must be legally eligible to use the Platform and must satisfy any minimum-age, employment, authorization or account requirements that apply to the relevant service. Where an organization invites employees or contractors, the organization is responsible for ensuring that invitations are appropriate and that access is consistent with its own policies and legal obligations. The Platform should not be used to circumvent age restrictions or other applicable legal requirements.

3.3 Accurate registration information

Users and organization representatives should provide information that is accurate, current and not misleading when creating or maintaining an account. This may include organization names, work email addresses, names, role assignments and other information reasonably required for account administration. Material changes should be corrected through available account settings or by contacting the appropriate administrator or support channel.

3.4 Compliance with applicable requirements

Each organization and user remains responsible for complying with laws, regulations, employment obligations, contractual commitments and professional standards applicable to their use of the Platform. Access to a task-management or collaboration feature does not relieve a user of obligations relating to confidential information, personal data, intellectual property, records retention, workplace conduct or regulated business activities.

ORGANIZATIONAL WORKSPACES

Organization Registration and Workspace Creation

4.1 Creating an organization workspace

The intended organization-registration workflow allows an authorized representative to create an organizational workspace by supplying the information requested during registration. The information may include an organization name, a contact address and administrator details. Registration alone does not establish that a company has been independently verified or that the person registering has been legally authorized. Any verification process must be clearly described if introduced into the live service.

4.2 Organization information and administration

An organization should ensure that workspace information is accurate and that only appropriately authorized personnel can change important settings. Depending on the features implemented, administrators may be able to manage departments, employees, designations, projects, role assignments and other workspace settings. These administrative capabilities should be used for legitimate organizational purposes and in accordance with applicable privacy, employment and information-security obligations.

4.3 Multiple workspaces and organizational boundaries

Where the service supports multiple organizations or workspaces, each organization must use its own permitted environment and access arrangements. A user must not attempt to access another organization's workspace, records, files or configuration without valid authorization. Shared devices, copied links, reused credentials or mistaken invitations do not create permission to view another organization's information.

4.4 Changes in organizational authority

Organizations should review administrator access when personnel change roles, leave employment or cease to act on the organization's behalf. The organization should maintain an appropriate process for transferring administrative responsibilities, correcting workspace details and removing obsolete access. Any transfer of control remains subject to applicable contracts, law and any technical limitations of the service.

ACCOUNT RESPONSIBILITIES

User Accounts, Credentials and Account Security

5.1 Individual accounts

Each person should use the account assigned to that person and should provide truthful information during account creation or activation. Accounts should not be shared between several individuals where the service is designed for individual access. Shared credentials make it difficult to determine who performed an action and may expose organizational information to unauthorized persons.

5.2 Protection of credentials

Users should protect passwords, verification codes, recovery methods, authentication devices and other credentials from disclosure. Credentials should not be transmitted through insecure channels or stored where unauthorized persons can readily obtain them. Users should use appropriate device protections and should sign out from shared devices when finished. Any password-strength, multifactor authentication or session-management requirement must reflect controls actually implemented in the live service.

5.3 Suspected compromise and unauthorized activity

If a user suspects that an account has been compromised, credentials have been exposed or unauthorized activity has occurred, the user should promptly notify the organization's designated administrator and use any available account-recovery or reporting procedure. The organization should investigate the issue and take appropriate steps to restrict access, preserve relevant evidence and reduce further exposure. Support availability and response commitments, if any, must be established separately.

5.4 Account activity and responsibility

Users should review significant account activity and avoid actions that allow other people to operate through their accounts. Organizations should establish clear onboarding and offboarding procedures, including the timely removal of accounts when access is no longer appropriate. Responsibility for a particular incident will depend on the facts, the controls in place, the parties' conduct and applicable law; it should not be presumed solely from the existence of an account.

WORKSPACE GOVERNANCE

Organization Administrators and Account Ownership

6.1 Administrator responsibilities

Organization administrators are expected to manage workspaces responsibly, assign roles only where appropriate, maintain current organizational information and review access when business requirements change. They should communicate relevant workspace policies to employees and contractors, including rules governing confidential information, project participation, acceptable use and the handling of organizational records.

6.2 Authority over employee access

Subject to applicable law and the organization's contractual and employment obligations, authorized administrators may manage employee invitations, project participation, role assignments and available workspace permissions. Such authority should be exercised only for legitimate organizational purposes. An administrator must not use administrative access to unlawfully discriminate against, harass, surveil or otherwise harm users.

6.3 Organization ownership of business records

The organization should determine who within the business has authority to manage its workspace, work records and organizational content. An employee's ability to create or edit a record does not, by itself, determine ownership of the underlying business information. Ownership and permitted use of work product remain subject to applicable law, employment arrangements, customer contracts and the intellectual-property provisions of this agreement.

6.4 Administrative disputes

If a dispute arises concerning who controls an organization workspace, the parties should seek to resolve it through their authorized organizational representatives and any applicable contractual process. A platform operator may require reasonable evidence of authority before considering a request to change administrative control, subject to applicable law. The operator should not arbitrarily transfer an organization's workspace to a person whose authority cannot be reasonably established.

ACCESS CONTROL

User Roles, Permissions and Access Controls

7.1 Role-based access

IT TaskFlow is intended to support differentiated responsibilities for roles such as organization administrators, HR personnel, managers, project managers, team leads, testing personnel and employees. The exact roles and permissions depend on the features implemented and the configuration selected by the organization. A role name alone does not confer legal authority to access information that the assigned permissions do not permit.

7.2 Least-privilege principle

Organizations should grant each user only the access reasonably necessary for the user's assigned responsibilities. Sensitive administrative functions should be restricted to appropriately authorized individuals. Permissions should be reviewed periodically and when employees change projects, roles or departments. Users must not exploit interface defects, guessable identifiers, exposed links or configuration errors to obtain unauthorized access.

7.3 Project-specific visibility

The intended project experience may display project information according to task assignments, explicit project access and role permissions. For example, an employee may see a project in the Projects menu when the employee has a qualifying task assignment or another expressly authorized basis for access. Multiple tasks within the same project need not create duplicate project entries. Access may change when assignments are removed or permissions are revoked. These are intended product rules and do not guarantee a security control unless the implemented service enforces them.

7.4 Responsibility for permission configuration

Administrators should verify that project membership, task assignments and access rules reflect the organization's actual requirements. Users should report unexpected access to information rather than copy, download or distribute it. Any reported permission issue should be investigated using the technical and organizational procedures available for the relevant service version.

EMPLOYEE LIFECYCLE

Employee Onboarding and Human Resources Workflows

8.1 Employee information and onboarding

Where employee-onboarding features are available, an organization may use them to coordinate onboarding activities, record workflow progress, assign responsibilities and organize documents associated with employment processes. The organization remains responsible for determining which information it may lawfully collect, who may access that information, how long records should be retained and whether additional notices or consents are required.

8.2 Accuracy and fairness

Organizations should ensure that employee information entered into the Platform is accurate, relevant and appropriate for the stated purpose. Sensitive information should not be entered into ordinary task descriptions or general collaboration areas unless the organization has a lawful basis and suitable access restrictions. Decisions affecting recruitment, employment, compensation, promotion or termination must be made in accordance with applicable law and appropriate human review.

8.3 Employee accounts and role changes

Organizations should ensure that employee accounts are created, updated and deactivated through authorized procedures. When an employee changes departments, responsibilities or projects, access should be adjusted to match the revised role. When employment or engagement ends, the organization should promptly review and revoke access that is no longer authorized, while preserving records that must lawfully be retained.

8.4 Employment decisions and recordkeeping

Workflow status, task completion, meeting attendance and reports produced through a productivity platform may provide only part of the information relevant to an employment decision. Organizations should not treat incomplete or inaccurate system records as conclusive proof of an employee's performance or conduct. They remain responsible for providing appropriate context, correcting errors and following fair internal procedures.

PROJECT MANAGEMENT

Project Creation, Assignment and Project Visibility

9.1 Project creation and administration

Authorized users may, where supported, create projects, define objectives, assign project managers, add permitted participants and maintain project information. Project descriptions, schedules, milestones and membership should accurately reflect the organization's working arrangements. Users should not create projects that impersonate another organization, misrepresent authority or facilitate unlawful activity.

9.2 Assignment and participation

Project managers and other authorized users may assign work or grant project access according to the organization's processes and the permissions available in the service. 9.3 Visibility of project information

A user should access project information only where the user has the necessary permission. Project visibility may depend on task assignments, membership, organization-level privileges or other explicit access rules. The fact that a project name appears in a menu does not authorize the user to access every document, conversation or record associated with it. Access restrictions should be enforced by the live service, not merely by hiding interface elements.

9.4 Project completion and closure

An organization may mark a project completed, archived or inactive when supported. Project closure should be coordinated with outstanding tasks, records retention, access reviews and any contractual obligations to customers or business partners. Closing a project should not be assumed to delete its information unless the applicable service expressly states that result.

WORK ASSIGNMENTS AND RECORDS

Task Management, Deadlines and Work Records

10.1 Task creation and assignment

Task-management features may allow authorized users to create work items, define descriptions, set priorities, select assignees, establish due dates, record status changes and link tasks to projects. Users should make task descriptions sufficiently clear to communicate the expected outcome and should avoid including information unrelated to the task's purpose.

10.2 Task status and completion

Task statuses are intended to communicate workflow progress. A status such as “In Progress,” “Blocked” or “Completed” should be used consistently with the organization's agreed process. Marking a task completed may indicate that a workflow step has been reported as finished; it does not necessarily certify the quality, legal sufficiency or acceptance of the underlying work by a customer or another party.

10.3 Deadlines, dependencies and delays

Users should set reasonable deadlines and communicate dependencies or obstacles through available channels. A date displayed in the Platform may depend on the configured time zone, device settings or service behavior. Organizations should clarify which time zone and business calendar apply to material deadlines. A reminder or notification should not be treated as a substitute for a user's responsibility to understand assigned work.

10.4 Work history and record accuracy

Comments, status changes, assignment histories and task timestamps may be used to coordinate work or review project progress. Users should not knowingly falsify task records, impersonate another user or manipulate work history to mislead an organization. Where an error occurs, the organization should use available correction procedures and preserve relevant records where legally required.

QUALITY AND TESTING WORKFLOWS

Testing, Bug Tracking and Software Development Workflows

12.1 Testing and issue records

Where testing and bug-tracking features are available, users may record defects, describe reproduction steps, attach permitted evidence, assign severity or priority and track resolution. 12.2 Handling of test data and evidence

Users must not upload production credentials, authentication secrets, personal information or confidential customer data into test records unless such processing is authorized, necessary and supported by appropriate safeguards. Screenshots and diagnostic files should be reviewed for unintended disclosure before upload.

12.3 Security testing and authorization

Testing must be limited to systems, applications and environments that the tester is authorized to assess. Users must not use IT TaskFlow to coordinate unauthorized intrusion, credential theft, destructive testing or disruption of systems belonging to others. Security assessments should follow the scope, approval process and safeguards agreed with the system owner.

12.4 Defect resolution and acceptance

Recording a defect or changing its status does not guarantee that the issue has been fixed, independently verified or accepted by a customer. The responsible team should follow its agreed testing, review and release procedures. Organizations remain responsible for deciding whether software is suitable for deployment and for maintaining any required quality assurance evidence.

INFORMATION AND REPORTING

Reports, Analytics and Generated Information

13.1 Purpose of reports

Organizations should interpret metrics in context, validate material conclusions and avoid treating automated summaries as the sole basis for consequential decisions.

13.3 Exports and downstream use

If export features are available, users must ensure that exported records are shared only with authorized recipients. Downloaded reports may no longer benefit from the access controls of the Platform. Organizations should protect exported files, maintain appropriate retention practices and verify information before relying on it in financial, contractual, employment or regulatory decisions.
